# Parent & Guardian Guide — Grade 4 Mathematics Pilot

Status: **Content deliverable (WS4/WS6-facing). Plan only.** A plain-language, Urdu-first guide for
the parents and guardians of children in the Grade 4 Mathematics pilot. It accompanies the
[curriculum](GRADE4_MATH_CURRICULUM.md); it is **content**, not a product feature.

> This guide is written to be translated/rendered Urdu-first for families. English is shown here as the
> working reference. Nothing here asks a child or family to share personal information online.

---

## 1. What your child is learning

Your child is learning **Grade 4 Mathematics** in **Urdu, with English support**, aligned to the
national curriculum. Over the pilot they will cover:

- **Numbers** — reading, writing, comparing, and rounding numbers up to 100,000.
- **Add & subtract** — larger numbers, and everyday word problems.
- **Multiply & divide** — tables, bigger multiplication, sharing, and remainders.
- **Fractions** — equal parts, equivalent fractions, comparing, and adding/subtracting.
- **Measurement** — length, weight, capacity, and telling the time.
- **Shapes** — lines, angles, 2D shapes, and symmetry.
- **Data** — reading picture graphs and bar graphs.

Lessons are **short (about 15–25 minutes)**, **spoken aloud in Urdu** (your child can listen, not just
read), and can work **offline** once downloaded.

---

## 2. How the learning works (what to expect)

- **A patient AI helper guides each lesson** — it explains, gives examples, and offers hints. It
  **never just gives the answer**; it helps your child think. It is **not a person** and never pretends
  to be one.
- **Mistakes are normal and welcome.** The lessons expect wrong answers and turn them into learning —
  your child will not be shamed, ranked, or graded harshly.
- **A real mentor (teacher) is involved.** Important checks and the final review are done by a **human
  mentor**, not automatically by the computer.
- **If your child is stuck or upset, a real person is reached.** Safety comes first, always.

---

## 3. How you can help at home (no maths expertise needed)

You do **not** need to be good at maths to help. The best support is everyday and spoken:

| Topic | A 2-minute thing you can do |
| --- | --- |
| Numbers | Ask your child to read house numbers, page numbers, or prices aloud. |
| Add & subtract | Work out change together at a shop. |
| Multiply | Count things in equal groups (eggs in a tray, chairs in rows). |
| Divide | Share snacks or rotis equally and ask "how many each, how many left?" |
| Fractions | Cut food into equal parts and name the fraction eaten (half, quarter). |
| Measurement | Measure height in cm; compare bottle sizes and packet weights. |
| Time | Ask your child to tell the time at meals. |
| Shapes | Point out corners (right angles), circles, and rectangles at home. |
| Data | Tally the family's favourite fruits and compare. |

Each lesson also gives your child **one short homework task** — usually something like the ideas above.
Please encourage it, but keep it light and positive.

---

## 4. Encouraging your child the right way

- **Praise effort, not just being "clever."** "You kept trying — well done" helps more than "you're so
  smart."
- **Let them struggle a little.** Being stuck for a moment is how learning happens; the app is built
  for that.
- **Keep sessions short and regular.** A little every day beats a long session once a week.
- **Celebrate finishing a lesson**, not just getting everything right.

---

## 5. Safety, privacy, and your rights

- **A human is always reachable.** If your child seems upset or asks for help beyond the maths, a
  trained mentor/safeguarding person is contacted. Your child's wellbeing comes before any lesson.
- **Your child's information is protected.** The pilot collects only what is needed to help them learn,
  and it is kept safely. You will be told clearly what is collected and asked for your **consent**
  before your child takes part.
- **No selling, no ads, no strangers.** There is no advertising, and there is no open chat with
  strangers. The AI helper only uses approved lesson content.
- **You can ask questions or opt out** at any time by contacting the pilot mentor/coordinator.
- **The app is designed to be usable by every child** — including those who find reading hard (it reads
  aloud) — and works on a normal phone, even with a weak internet connection.

---

## 6. During the pilot

- Your child will use the app **at the learning center, with mentors present** (and, in later stages,
  possibly at home).
- If anything worries you — technical, educational, or about your child's comfort — **tell the mentor
  or coordinator**. Your feedback shapes the programme.
- There is **no high-stakes exam** in the pilot. The goal is to help your child learn and enjoy maths,
  and to learn how to make the programme better.

---

## 7. Questions parents often ask

**Do I need internet all the time?** No — lessons download and then work offline.

**My child can't read well yet. Can they still use it?** Yes — everything is read aloud in Urdu.

**Is the AI going to replace the teacher?** No — a human mentor is central; the AI is a patient helper.

**Will my child be compared to others?** No — there is no ranking or public score. Learning is private
and personal.

**What if my child gives a wrong answer many times?** The app gives gentle hints, then a real mentor is
brought in — your child is never left stuck or upset.
